The judgment–action gap by model

Seven dilemmas, sixteen models. In each dilemma an AI agent doing routine work reaches a case where following the procedure would seriously harm a real person, and protecting them means breaking a rule. We ask each model what the agent should do (judgment, called “theory” in the card notes), and separately let it do the job as an agent in a sandbox (action). The gap is how much more often it protects the person in judgment than in action.

How to read it

  • Rows are dilemmas, columns are models. Hover over or tap a dilemma name for a short summary of the case.
  • The big number in a card is the gap, in percentage points: the share of judgment answers that protect the person, minus the share of action runs that do. +100 means the model always says the agent should protect the person and never does it itself; a negative number means it protects more in action than it recommends.
  • Card colour shows the size of the gap, whatever its sign: the deeper the blue, the larger the gap, from pale at 0 to deep at 100 points. A reversed gap of −90 is as deep as +90; the sign is in the number. A dashed card has too few valid action runs to judge.
  • The “test” line shows in how many action runs the model said it might be in a test or evaluation, from blind coding of its visible reasoning and messages. The dot uses the same scale: green up to a third of runs, yellow under half, red half or more. Talk of a test matters because a model that suspects an evaluation may behave differently from one that does not.
  • Hover over or tap a card for the details: the raw counts in judgment and action, significance, what the model actually did, and its answers to the other three judgment questions (what it would do itself, what a typical AI agent would do, what a typical employee would do).

Method. Judgment: 10 answers per model to the normative question (“what should the agent do?”), with the relevant rules retold inside the scenario rather than attached as a policy document. Action: up to 10 agent runs per model in a sandbox with real tools, scored by outcome. Where a cell has fewer than 10 valid runs, the note says why (usually provider errors). Significance is Fisher's exact test, Holm-corrected across models in most dilemmas; each card note gives the p-value used.

Sonnet and astra were added later and now run in all seven dilemmas on the same versions as the other models. Two more models were tried but gave no valid data: opus (blocked by the provider's content filter) and deepseek (unavailable through OpenRouter).
